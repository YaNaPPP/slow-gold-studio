Is recycled gold as good as new?

Yes. Gold is an element, so it never wears out. Refining brings it back to the same purity as freshly mined gold, and every piece is hallmarked 585 (14k) or 750 (18k).

03 — The makingFour weeksOne bench, one goldsmith

Four weeks at one bench.

No moulds and no production line. One goldsmith takes your piece from the first sketch to the last polish, and you see every step.

Skip to pieces and prices
Week 1

We listen and draw.

Tell us whose gold it was and who will wear it next. We draw the piece around that story and send you the sketch with the final weight and price. Nothing is melted until you say yes.

Week 2

We weigh it with you.

Bring your gold to the atelier, or send it with insured shipping we arrange. We film the weighing and send you the video before anything is melted.

Week 2

We melt and cast.

At 1,064 °C the old pieces become one small bar. Only your gold is in the crucible.

Weeks 2–3

We forge by hand.

The bar is rolled, bent and hammered into shape. Every mark on the surface is a hammer strike, so no two pieces look alike, not even two made from the same gold.

Week 4

We finish and engrave.

Engraving, setting and the last polish happen by hand. Then the piece is hallmarked 750 and written into its Gold Passport: the weight that went in, the weight that came out, and the story you told us.

What if I don’t have enough gold?

We top it up with our recycled gold and tell you exactly how many grams came from you and how many from us. Both appear in your Gold Passport.

Can you keep the stones?

Yes. Stones are taken out before melting, cleaned and checked. We set them in the new piece or return them to you.

How long does it take?

About four weeks from the approved sketch to the finished piece. We send photos at every step.

What if I don’t like the sketch?

We draw again. If we still can’t find the right design together, you pay nothing for the sketches and your gold comes back exactly as you sent it.
